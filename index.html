Why RFQ on Canton

Public order books leak. Private quotes don't.

Every swap on a public chain broadcasts your intent before it executes. On Canton, negotiation happens between you and your counterparties. Nobody else sees it.

01 · pricing

Firm, not indicative

When a solver fills, they deliver at least the maker’s minimum. The terms you lock into settlement are the terms you get — slippage isn’t “reduced,” it’s structurally absent from the fill.

02 · privacy

No mempool, no MEV

Canton has no public mempool to snipe. Your RFQ is visible to participants on the desk, not to the world. Front-running needs an audience — Helvex doesn’t give it one.

03 · settlement

Atomic, both legs

Settlement is one Canton transaction: maker’s sell asset and solver’s buy asset move together, or neither moves. No half-fills, no escrow limbo after match.

How a trade works

Four steps. Atomic settlement.

Lock is its own ledger step. Settlement of both assets is a single atomic Canton transaction.

01

Request

Pick a pair and size. Helvex locks your sell amount in escrow so it cannot be spent twice.

02

Compare

Solvers see locked RFQs on the desk. Indicative reference quotes refresh; fills must meet your minimum.

03

Accept

A second verified account fills. First accepted fill wins; you cannot fill your own RFQ.

04

Settle

Both legs clear atomically. Activity shows the settlement; cancel or timeout returns a refund to available.

Privacy & settlement

Show the right people the right data.

Canton’s sub-transaction privacy means stakeholders only receive the contract views they need. Helvex uses that model for RFQ negotiation and DvP settlement.

  • Selective disclosure — your RFQ is not broadcast to a global mempool; it reaches the desk’s counterparties.
  • Atomic settlement — after a fill, both assets move in one Canton transaction, or neither does.
  • Locked funds — sell amounts in escrow cannot be spent, withdrawn, or reused on another RFQ until settle or refund.
  • Clear activity — settled trades land in Activity with a settlement reference you can reconcile.
Honest scope

What is — and isn’t — one transaction

Deposit and withdraw are separate ledger steps. Lock is its own transaction. Settlement of both swap legs is atomic. That is the guarantee that removes counterparty leg risk after a match.

FAQ

Common questions.

What is an RFQ on Helvex?
A request for quote is a posted trade: you state what you will sell and the minimum you will accept. Helvex locks the sell amount. Another verified account (the solver) fills it. There is no public order book.
Firm vs indicative — what’s the difference?
Reference quotes in the app are indicative and refresh (about 30 seconds). Once an RFQ is locked and a solver fills at your minimum, settlement uses those firm terms — not a drifting pool price.
Why is there no slippage?
You’re not walking an order book. Fill and settlement honor the accepted RFQ amounts. App fee is 0 bps today; Canton network traffic still costs a small amount of CC.
Loop wallet vs trading ID?
Loop holds your keys for deposit and withdraw. Your Helvex trading ID is the Canton party that posts, locks, fills, and settles. Connect Loop on the same network as the Helvex app you use.
Who sees my RFQ?
Locked RFQs are visible to counterparties on the Helvex desk so they can fill. There is no public mempool broadcast of your intent the way there is on many public chains.
Why is KYC required?
Helvex is a permissioned desk. Verification (government ID + live selfie) is required before activate, deposit, trade, or withdraw. Access also follows geo and sanctions controls.
Can I trade from an API?
Yes. After you activate and fund in the app, mint an HMAC API key for maker/solver automation. Deposit, withdraw, and activate stay in the website session. See the API integrator guide.
